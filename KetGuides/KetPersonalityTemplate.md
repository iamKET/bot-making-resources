### Personality template with notes
```
> LORE & SETTINGS

A brief overview of the world setting and any context or lore that's important to the plot. Keep it around 200 words max. If it runs longer than that, consider trimming it down or moving the extra details into Lorebook.


> OVERVIEW

- Name: 

- Age: 

- Job / Title: (pick one or both — I usually go with Job for modern settings and Title for historical ones)

> APPEARANCE

- Height: 

- Eyes: 

- Hair: 

- Body:

- Face:

- You can add more notable features here such as scars, beauty marks, piercings, or scent. Keep everything short and to the point — strictly appearance only, no need to elaborate on the 'vibe' unless it feels necessary for the character's personality.

> BACKGROUND
Again, keep it short and sweet — key moments only. Ask yourself these questions when writing the background:
- Does this affect the bot’s personality?
- Does this affect the bot’s relationship with user?
- Does this affect the bot’s relationship with any important NPCs?


> SIDE NPCs
Important NPCs only — age, gender, and who they are to the bot. Keep each bullet point to about 1–3 sentences max, unless it's a particularly important NPC, in which case you can go longer. If you want to add a lot of detail or a lot of NPCs, consider using Lorebook. 


> PERSONALITY

- Core Archetype: This is the core and baseline of your character. The AI tends to follow this the most strongly. If you think your bot is falling into certain tropey cliche shit, i'd recommend to just remove this Core Archetype

- Tags: Second most important. Be careful with overused tags like 'possessive' or 'controlling'. Aim for about 5–10 tags max — less is more. Again, if your bot is falling into cliche tropey action and talk like a cringy dark romance mafia, just remove this tag part or remove tags such as: possessive, controlling, dominant, blah blah. 

- Or you can also write a tag and how it apply to them. Example: "patient: he is very patient with children and people he love, can explain things over and over again until they understand without getting annoyed or angry at them, however for strangers he is not patient and often dissmissive."

- Surface: How the character acts outwardly. Use this to cover or further explain anything the tags didn't capture.

- Psych deeper dive: How the character's psychology on the inside — the side that only people they trust get to see. The depth of their personality. What part of them that they never show people, what are their beliefs, etc...

> WITH {{user}}

How they treat and view the user. In some scenarios it helps to separate how they act toward the user publicly vs. privately.
Keep it short and sweet — generally, how they view the user will naturally shape how they treat them in a way that fits their personality.


> GENERAL BEHAVIORS AND HABITS

Key habits, tics, daily routines.

> MOTIVATION

Short-term: What they want right now. This helps shape the current roleplay.
Long-term: What their end goal is, what they want out of life. This helps shape the roleplay's bigger picture.

> FEAR

Totally optional, but it's good to have 1–2 bullet points to show their vulnerability.


> POSSESSIONS

Optional. Pick 1–5 items that are meaningful to them — where they live, their daily vehicle, important objects they keep close.

> SEXUALITY

- Cock: Length and description. Balls, hair, LOL, idk
- Kinks: A list of kinks — pick a few that align with their personality. You can try throwing in something that goes against their personality, but it usually won't stick. The AI tends to default to whatever feels fitting for the character. 
- If you pick any less common kinks, it's worth adding a few lines explaining how they apply them in intimate scenes.
- A few bullet points on how you want them to act during sex — yeah, that's the sauce, LOL. Don't overthink it.
- Dirty talk style too, if you want. 

> SPEECH AND EXAMPLES

- Style: Overall speech style. maybe some speech quirks too
- A few example sentences — their beliefs, how they sound when angry or sad, their insult style, slang, how they talk to the user, how they talk to NPCs, etc. 
```

### Author General Notes
- I normally keep the bot's personality around 2k–3k tokens. The upper ceiling is around 3.5k — I'd avoid going over 4k. Cut repetitive information, rambling, and overly elaborate details. More tokens doesn't mean better quality. Honestly, the AI will skip over a lot of it — yes, all the information gets sent, but not all of it actually gets applied during roleplay. You know how you give an AI a long task and it only does half of it, then goes off and does something else entirely? It's pretty much the same thing here. Less is more — keep the key points.

- I know people like adding likes and dislikes. It's helpful sometimes, but with this format it's often already implied by the personality anyway. Sometimes I add it, sometimes I don't.

- If you have important notes for the AI, you can include an `> AI Guidelines` section after Speech and Examples. I normally find this unnecessary for my template, or for how I write bots in general.

- Never depict what {{user}} knows, does, or feels, etc... in the template — it makes zero sense. This is a guideline for your character, not the user.

- No need to add advanced prompts or jailbreak prompts to the personality. They're not effective here, and they can make your character too rigid or break character within a few messages.

- The more you try to "overly control" your character, the less flexible they become, which can make them feel robotic. Instead of writing exactly how they'd react in a specific scenario, write how they think, their coping mechanisms, and how their reactions shift depending on mood, etc.
